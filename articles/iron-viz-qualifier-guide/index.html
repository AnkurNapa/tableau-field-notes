Iron Viz: enter the qualifier with story, design and analysis

How Iron Viz works in 2026, the published judging criteria turned into a self-scoring sheet and how to find and fix the weakest of the three before you submit.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 3 Ankur Napa CommunityPublicDesign

Short answer

Iron Viz is Tableau's data visualisation competition. A global qualifier, open for about a month and usually in the second half of the year, picks three finalists. They then get 20 minutes on the Tableau Conference stage to build with the same data set in front of the audience. Judges score every qualifier entry on design, storytelling and analysis, with analysis as the tiebreaker. Most entries are not lost on skill. They are lost on one weak criterion the author never measured. Score your own draft against the published criteria a week before the deadline, fix the lowest one, then submit.

The 2026 championship was the 16th edition. At the time of writing I could not confirm the 2027 qualifier dates or theme. Watch the Iron Viz page and the Tableau Community Slack. Read the official rules for your country before you start: eligibility is set there, not in blog posts. There is also a separate Iron Viz: Student Edition for college students.

The symptom

A good-looking entry that does not make the top 10. The usual pattern in published top entries is a clear point of view carried by real analysis. The usual pattern in entries that miss is one of these:

  • a beautiful layout around a thin data set,
  • strong analysis with no story, so the reader has to work out the point,
  • a story that drifts off the theme,
  • text below 12 point or colour the only cue, which the design criteria name explicitly.

Measure it

Turn the published criteria into a scoring sheet. Score each line 0 (missing), 1 (partly) or 2 (clearly there). Ask someone who has not seen the viz to score it too.

Design

Criterion0 to 2
Visual elements add to understanding rather than distract
Interactivity and layout are user-friendly, instructed and purposeful
Charts present the data clearly
Charts have a title, summary or caption
Every chart contributes to the story
Accessible: colour-blind and low-vision friendly palette, little text in images, 12 point or larger

Analysis

Criterion0 to 2
Topic fits the theme
Data and calculations look accurate and clean
The viz shows real insight, not just description
Analysis supports the story
Analysis was mostly done in Tableau
Shows a broad range of Tableau capabilities

Storytelling

Criterion0 to 2
A clear story is told
The story moves from question to insight
Visuals and animations support the story
A unique idea or perspective
Complexity or nuance that lifts the vizzes
Holds interest to the end

Each section is out of 12. The lowest section is where your next week goes. Note that analysis breaks ties, so a draft with a weak analysis score is in more danger than its total suggests.

Then measure two practical things:

  1. The cold read. Show the viz to someone for 60 seconds, then take it away. Ask what it was about. If they describe the topic but not the finding, storytelling is the weak link.
  2. Load and interaction. Publish a hidden draft to Tableau Public and open it on a normal laptop on home broadband. If it takes long enough for you to notice, a judge will notice too.

Fix it

If design scored lowest

  • Delete a chart. Every chart must contribute to the story; the extra one rarely does.
  • Raise all text to 12 point or larger and check contrast at 4.5:1.
  • Add direct labels so colour is never the only cue.
  • Replace instructions-by-guesswork with one line of text that says what to select.

If analysis scored lowest

  • Add a comparison the reader cannot do in their head: a rate, an index, a rank, a share of total with an LOD that holds under filters.
  • Bring the data work into Tableau where you can: calculations, LODs and table calculations count as analysis. Note any heavy preparation done elsewhere.
  • Check the numbers against the source. Write down three totals and recompute them outside Tableau.
  • Use a feature that suits the data, not one that shows off. A parameter action for a what-if counts when the what-if matters.

If storytelling scored lowest

  • Write the finding as one sentence. Make it the title.
  • Order the charts as an argument: question, evidence, turn, conclusion.
  • Add a personal reason for the topic. Finalists' own notes on their 2026 entries talk about family, culture and long-held curiosity, not just the data set.
  • Cut the scroll. If the end of the viz says something new, move it up.

Prove it

Score the draft again after the fixes, with the same reader if you can. An illustrative before and after for one draft:

SectionBeforeAfter
Design9 / 1210 / 12
Analysis6 / 1210 / 12
Storytelling8 / 1211 / 12

These numbers are illustrative, not measured. Your own sheet is the evidence. If any section did not move, you fixed the wrong thing. After the results, request your real scores and feedback with the form on the Iron Viz page and compare them with your own. The gap teaches you how the judges read.

Where this breaks

  • The theme is wide on purpose. "Food and drink" let one entrant viz confectionery history and another space farming. Wide themes punish generic topics. Pick a narrow corner you know.
  • Data sourcing eats the month. Gather and check the data in week one. Design starts in week two at the latest.
  • Heavy external analysis. The criteria ask for analysis mostly produced in Tableau. A Python notebook that does all the thinking weakens that score.
  • Last-day publishing. Tableau Public uploads can be slow near a deadline. Submit a day early.
  • Rules vary by country. Prize eligibility depends on the official rules for where you live. Read them.

Next

Whether or not you reach the top 10, your Iron Viz entry is a ready-made talk. Turn it into one with Speak at a user group. Add it to your portfolio as an anchor.

On the brewhouse floor

The 2026 qualifier theme was food and drink, which is a reminder that beverages make strong Iron Viz material. A distillery story might follow one cask from filling to bottling: the strength falling year by year, the spirit lost to evaporation (the angel's share) and the flavour building as it goes. Analysis is the volume and strength curve. Story is why that one cask matters. Design is a warehouse layout the reader can walk through. For the data side, see a cask maturation and angel's share dashboard.
