Running a centre of excellence

Use Tableau Blueprint to run Tableau as a programme, not a product: a sponsor, a small centre of excellence and four tracks of work in agility, proficiency, community and governance, with a governance model that fits how the company actually works.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 4 Ankur Napa ServerCloudCommunityCertification

Short answer

Tableau Blueprint is Tableau's free, published method for becoming a data-driven organisation. It is built on what worked at thousands of customers. It treats Tableau as a programme with four tracks of work:

  • Agility: deploying, monitoring and maintaining the platform
  • Proficiency: educating users, measuring adoption and spreading good practice
  • Community: growing users and promoting analytics through communication and support
  • Governance: the controls, roles and repeatable processes that make people trust the data

Before any of those come two foundations: an analytics strategy and an executive sponsor with a cross-functional project team. The group that runs the four tracks for everyone else is the centre of excellence (CoE). Tableau Blueprint spells it Center of Excellence. Same thing.

The decision that shapes everything else is the governance model. Blueprint names three: Centralized, Delegated and Self-Governing. Most companies start nearer the first and move towards the second.

The decision

ModelWho buildsWho publishes data sourcesFitsBreaks when
CentralizedIT or a BI team builds data sources and dashboards. Most users are ViewersThe central teamSensitive data, early programmes, regulated teamsThe backlog grows faster than the team
DelegatedAnalysts in business teams build on certified sourcesCentral team plus named data stewards and site or project adminsMost mid-size companies once the basics workStewards are named but never given time
Self-GoverningAnyone with a licence, alongside certified contentBusiness and IT together, with a known certification processMature programmes with strong collaborationCertification becomes a rubber stamp

You can mix models by topic. Blueprint's own example: delegated metadata management with centralised security and permissions.

Measure the programme with a handful of numbers reviewed monthly by the steering committee: active users by role against licences, share of views served from certified sources, stale content count, refresh success rate and time to answer a support request. Admin Insights on Cloud or the repository on Server supply all but the last.

Questions to ask the client

  1. Who is the executive sponsor? What decision did they make about analytics in the last quarter?
  2. What is the analytics strategy in one sentence? Which business outcomes will it be judged on?
  3. Which data is sensitive enough that only a central team may publish it?
  4. Who owns each major data domain: sales, stock, finance, people?
  5. How do new users learn Tableau today? How long until they publish something useful?
  6. Is there an internal community: a chat channel, a user group, champions in departments?
  7. How is content certified today? Who can remove a certification?
  8. What would make the CoE redundant in three years? A good answer exists.

Review checklist

  • A named executive sponsor and a steering committee that meets on a fixed rhythm.
  • A written governance model, by topic, with the reason for each choice.
  • Data owners and stewards named for every certified data source.
  • Certification criteria written down and applied the same way every time.
  • Production projects locked. One sandbox with a written archive rule.
  • Role-based learning paths for Viewers, Explorers and Creators.
  • Champions in each major department with time in their objectives for the role.
  • Adoption metrics reported monthly from Admin Insights or the repository.
  • The Blueprint self-assessment completed at the start and repeated yearly.

Anti-patterns

  • A CoE of one. The Tableau admin who also builds dashboards, trains users and answers tickets. That is a person, not a centre.
  • Governance as a gate. Every workbook waits for central approval. The business routes around it with spreadsheets and the CoE governs an empty site.
  • Licences as the measure. Counting licences sold instead of decisions made with data.
  • Community as an afterthought. No user group, no champions, no place to ask a question. People stop asking and start guessing.
  • Copying Blueprint whole. Blueprint is a menu. Pick the parts that solve the problems you actually have.

On the certification

This step maps to the Salesforce Certified Tableau Consultant exam, which tests leading the design of an analytics solution with customers. It also maps to the Salesforce Certified Tableau Architect exam. The official Consultant guide lists 60 scored items and a 63 per cent pass mark. Check both current guides on Trailhead Academy for objective lists.

Next on the roadmap

This is the last step of the Server and Cloud track. For the community side of a CoE, the Public track covers user groups, Tableau Ambassadors and the wider DataFam on the roadmap. If the CoE is about to move platforms, revisit Cloud or Server: the deployment decision.

On the brewhouse floor

A brewery already runs a centre of excellence. It is called the quality lab. The lab sets the methods, certifies the numbers that go on the certificate of analysis, trains the brewers to take a sample properly and still lets the cellar team run their own quick checks. A Tableau CoE should work the same way: certify the few numbers everyone must agree on, such as hectolitres packaged and brewhouse yield. Let the floor explore the rest. The lab does not brew the beer. The CoE should not build every dashboard.
