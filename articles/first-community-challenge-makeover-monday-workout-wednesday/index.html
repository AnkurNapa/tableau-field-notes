Your first community challenge: Makeover Monday, WOW or B2VB

Rehearse a makeover on a fictional pie chart, then enter a real weekly challenge: Back 2 Viz Basics, Workout Wednesday or Makeover Monday. Where each one lives in 2026, how to submit and how to get feedback that helps.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 1 Ankur Napa CommunityPublicDesktop

Short answer

Pick the challenge that matches what you want to learn. Back 2 Viz Basics teaches one chart type at a time. Workout Wednesday teaches technique: you rebuild someone else's viz exactly. Makeover Monday teaches judgement: you get a chart and its data and make it better. Enter one, publish to Tableau Public, submit the link where the project asks for it and post with the hashtag. The learning is in the comparison afterwards, so read five other entries every week.

What you will build

Two things. First, a ten-minute rehearsal on data you already have: turn a pie chart of Harbour Goods sales by store into a sorted bar of each store's share. Second, a real entry to one live challenge, published and submitted.

Practice data: harbour-goods-orders-clean.csv, 55 orders from a fictional retailer for July to September 2026. If you built Net Sales in Publish to Tableau Public, reuse that workbook.

Before you start

  • Tableau Desktop Public Edition and a Tableau Public account.
  • An account on the Tableau Community Slack so you can read #back-to-viz-basics, #workout-wednesday and #community-viz-feedback.
  • One hour a week you can protect. Challenges reward a habit more than a heroic weekend.

Steps

1. Build the "before" chart

  1. Connect to the CSV and create Net Sales:
[quantity] * [unit_price] * (1 - [discount])
  1. On the Marks card, change the mark type from Automatic to Pie.
  2. Drag store_id to Color and Net Sales to Angle.

Look at it honestly. Chennai South and Mumbai West are two slices of almost exactly the same size. Can you tell which is bigger? Nobody can. That is the problem a makeover fixes.

2. Make it over

  1. Duplicate the sheet: right-click the tab > Duplicate.
  2. Change the mark type to Bar. Remove store_id from Color and drag it to Rows. Remove Net Sales from Angle and drag it to Columns.
  3. Right-click SUM(Net Sales) on Columns > Quick Table Calculation > Percent of Total. The pill gets a triangle to show it is now a table calculation.
  4. Select Sort Descending on the toolbar.
  5. Select Show Mark Labels on the toolbar. The labels show the percentage, because they follow the pill on Columns.
  6. Give it a title that says the finding: A quarter of Harbour Goods sales came from Bengaluru South.

4. Enter it

Back 2 Viz Basics

  1. Open the 2026 challenge table and download the current data set.
  2. Build the chart type the prompt names. Nothing more.
  3. Publish to Tableau Public.
  4. Submit the link in the B2VB tracking form, then post with #B2VB.

Workout Wednesday

  1. Open the latest Tableau challenge on workout-wednesday.com. New to it? Start with WOW Essentials.
  2. Read the requirements list twice. The challenge is to match it, so the list is your spec.
  3. Download the data from the challenge page, build, then publish to Tableau Public.
  4. Leave a comment on the challenge with your Tableau Public link and post with #WOW2026.
  5. On Saturday, read the solution walkthrough at the bottom of the challenge and note one thing the author did differently.

Makeover Monday

  1. On the portal, open Datasets and download this week's data. Read the original article first.
  2. Write one sentence: what should the reader understand that the original chart hid?
  3. Build the smallest viz that makes that sentence obvious, then publish to Tableau Public.
  4. Select Submit Viz on the portal and post with #MakeoverMonday on LinkedIn or X.

5. Ask for feedback the right way

Post in #community-viz-feedback with three things: the link, what you were trying to show and the one part you are unsure about. "Any feedback?" gets politeness. "Does the colour legend fight the title?" gets help.

Check it worked

Your rehearsal bar chart should show these shares, computed in Python from the CSV:

store_idStoreShare of SUM(Net Sales)
ST-003Bengaluru South26.70%
ST-019Delhi North21.32%
ST-014Pune West19.12%
ST-007Chennai South12.46%
ST-011Mumbai West12.37%
ST-022Kolkata East8.03%

The six add to 100.00%. Chennai South beats Mumbai West by 0.09 points, about 140 rupees on a total of 153,065.80. The pie could not show that. The bar does.

For the real entry, success is simpler: your viz is public, your link is in the submission form or comments and you have read five other entries.

Where this breaks

  • Percent of total moves when you filter. Table calculations run after filters. Filter out a store and the other shares grow. Use a context filter or a FIXED LOD if the share must stay fixed; that is a Level 2 topic.
  • Old data links. The data.world Open Data Community closed on 13 July 2026. Tutorials that point there are dead ends. Use the project's own site.
  • Copying instead of rebuilding. Workout Wednesday solutions are downloadable. Opening one before you try teaches you nothing. Look only after you are stuck for an hour.
  • Overreaching in week one. Makeover Monday rewards one clear point. A dashboard with nine charts is not a makeover.
  • Silent entries. Publishing without the hashtag or the form means nobody sees it. The feedback loop is the whole value.

On the brewhouse floor

Whisky marketing loves a pie chart: share of a blend by cask type, share of sales by age statement. Two slices of 12 and 13 per cent look identical. Those are exactly the decisions a blender cares about. A sorted bar of share, labelled with the percentage, settles the argument in one glance.
