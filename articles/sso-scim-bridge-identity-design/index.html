Identity, SSO and private data

Design how people sign in to Tableau with SAML or OIDC, how users and groups arrive with SCIM and how Tableau Cloud reaches data behind the firewall with Tableau Bridge. Three decisions, one identity provider.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 4 Ankur Napa ServerCloudSecurityCertification

Short answer

Make the company's identity provider (IdP) the single source of truth for three things:

  1. Who can sign in. Single sign-on with SAML or OIDC. MFA is enforced at the IdP. On Tableau Cloud, MFA is a contractual requirement, so if you do not use SSO you use Tableau with MFA.
  2. Who exists and which groups they are in. SCIM provisioning pushes users and group membership from the IdP. Tableau Cloud supports it. Tableau Server does from 2025.3.
  3. What attributes travel with them. SAML attributes or OIDC claims can feed USERATTRIBUTE() for row-level security.

Private data is a separate decision that only Cloud has to make. Tableau Bridge clients run inside your network, connect out to Tableau Cloud and carry live queries and extract refreshes for sources Cloud cannot reach. Run them in pools, never alone.

The identity store is chosen at install and is hard to change later, so decide before you install.

Reference design

Bridge planning numbers from Tableau's guide: 16 live queries per client, 10 concurrent refreshes per client on Bridge schedules (configurable), one client per computer and no more than 10 clients under one Windows service account. For up to 10 parallel refreshes Tableau suggests 8 vCPU, 32 GB RAM and 300 GB NVMe disk per client. Live queries through Bridge time out after 15 minutes. Bridge also runs as a Linux container on Red Hat and Amazon Linux.

Map each pool to the network domains it serves, so a client in the plant network is never asked to reach the head office database.

Questions to ask the client

  1. Which IdP do you use? Who can change its applications and claims?
  2. Is MFA enforced at the IdP for every user who will reach Tableau?
  3. Do you need row-level security from IdP attributes, or from tables in the data?
  4. Do contractors or partners sign in through a different IdP? On Server, that is an identity pools question.
  5. Will you embed Tableau in a portal? How do those users sign in? Does the IdP allow iframes?
  6. Which data sources sit inside the network? What are their sizes and refresh windows?
  7. Who owns the Bridge machines, their patches and their drivers?
  8. Who is the break-glass admin if the IdP is down?

Review checklist

  • At least one site admin can sign in with Tableau with MFA, as a documented break-glass account.
  • SSO has been tested with one provisioned user before switching everyone's authentication type.
  • The username attribute matches the username format in your entitlement tables.
  • SCIM owns users and groups. Nobody edits them by hand in Tableau, because the IdP overwrites the change.
  • The SCIM secret is created by a long-lived admin account. On Cloud it stops working if that admin's role changes or they leave.
  • On Server, SCIM has an on-premises connector in the IdP if the server is behind a firewall.
  • Bridge runs at least two clients per pool, in service mode, with drivers matched across clients.
  • USERATTRIBUTE() is not used on any source that refreshes through Bridge. It does not work there.

Anti-patterns

  • SSO without SCIM. People sign in through the IdP but leavers keep their Tableau licence, because nobody removes them.
  • Hand-editing SCIM users. The next sync quietly undoes it.
  • One Bridge client on a laptop. It sleeps, it travels and it takes every refresh with it.
  • RLS on display names. Display names change at marriage, at typo fixes and at HR system migrations. Use a stable username or ID.
  • Service accounts as people. A shared login for a department breaks auditing, USERNAME()-based security and SCIM all at once.

On the brewhouse floor

A distillery's bonded warehouse records sit on a server in the site office, by law and by habit. Two Bridge clients on small VMs in that office carry the cask inventory to Tableau Cloud each night. The excise team signs in with the same company login and MFA prompt they use for email. When the warehouse manager retires, SCIM removes her access the day HR closes her record. Nobody has to remember. See a cask maturation and angel's share dashboard for the dashboard those records feed.
