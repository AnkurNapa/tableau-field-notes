Speak at a user group: turn a project into a twenty-minute session

How to get a speaking slot at a Tableau User Group, build a twenty-minute session around one project, rehearse it against the clock and leave the room with something they can reuse.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 3 Ankur Napa CommunityPublic

Short answer

Pick one project you finished, one problem it solved and one technique the audience can copy on Monday. Apply through the speaker form on the Tableau User Group site or write to the group's leaders directly. Build twenty minutes as four blocks: the problem, the build, the result and the takeaway. Rehearse twice with a timer and a recording. Keep a pre-built copy of every demo step. Most first talks fail on time, not content.

How you get a slot

  • The speaker form. The Start a Group page on usergroups.tableau.com has an Apply to be a speaker link for local and virtual events. TUG leaders review the submissions.
  • The leaders themselves. Every group page lists its organisers. A short message with a title, a two-line abstract and your Tableau Public link gets read. Offer two possible months.
  • The show-and-tell route. Many groups keep short slots for community work. Five minutes on your Makeover Monday entry is a fine first step.
  • Virtual groups. Industry and interest groups meet online and need speakers from everywhere, so your time zone is rarely the barrier.

The symptom

A talk that runs out of time on slide 14. The demo freezes on the venue's Wi-Fi. Questions arrive just as the meeting ends. The audience remembers you were nervous and forgets what you showed. None of this is about Tableau skill. It is about a talk that was never measured before it met an audience.

Measure it

Measure three things before the day.

1. Time per block. Draft the session in four blocks and give each a budget:

BlockBudgetWhat goes in it
The problem3 minWho had the question, why the old answer failed
The build10 minTwo or three steps in Tableau, live or recorded
The result3 minWhat changed, with one number
The takeaway2 minThe one technique they can reuse plus where to find your workbook
Questions2 minLeave this empty on purpose

Run the whole thing with a timer, out loud, standing up if you will stand on the day. Write the actual time beside each budget.

2. Words on slides. Count words on your busiest slide. If the audience is reading, they are not listening.

3. Demo risk. For each live step, ask: what happens if this fails? Note any step that needs the internet, a sign-in, a large extract or a feature that only works on your machine.

An example measured first run, for a talk on remaking a pie chart as a sorted bar with the Harbour Goods sample data:

BlockBudgetFirst run
The problem3 min5 min
The build10 min14 min
The result3 min2 min
The takeaway2 min1 min
Questions2 min0 min

These numbers are illustrative. The shape is typical: the problem and the build overrun, the takeaway gets squeezed, questions vanish.

Fix it

  • Cut the problem to one story. One person, one question, one bad chart. Context beyond that belongs in the follow-up post.
  • Cut the build to the steps that teach. Show the calculation, the table calculation setting and the sort. Skip connecting to the CSV; everyone has seen it.
  • Make every demo step recoverable. Save one workbook per stage (01-before.twbx, 02-calc.twbx, 03-final.twbx). If a step fails, open the next file and keep talking. Download any Tableau Public viz you plan to show and open it locally in case the network drops.
  • Put the takeaway on its own slide. One sentence and one link: your Tableau Public viz with downloads on, so people can open the workbook.
  • Protect the questions. If you finish early, nobody minds.
  • Use invented or public data only. User group sessions are often recorded and shared. Treat the recording as public, because it usually is.
  • Ask about recording before the day. Leaders decide whether a session is recorded. Agree it in advance. Blank any screen you do not want published.

Prove it

Rehearse again with the cuts and record it on your laptop. Compare:

MeasureFirst runSecond run
Total time22 min18 min
Busiest slide64 words18 words
Live steps with no fallback40
Minutes left for questions02

These numbers are illustrative too. Your own recording is the proof. Watch it once at normal speed. If you would lose interest in the build, so will the room.

On the day, after the talk:

  1. Post the workbook link and slides in the group's channel or on the event page.
  2. Write a short post with the group hashtag #TableauUserGroup and thank the leaders.
  3. Ask one attendee what was unclear. Fix that slide before you give the talk again.

Where this breaks

  • Venue Wi-Fi and screen resolution. Test the projector or the meeting platform's screen share before the room fills. Set Tableau's dashboard to a size that reads at 1080p.
  • Client and employer material. A real dashboard from work, even blurred, can breach your contract. Rebuild the idea on public or invented data.
  • Talking to the expert in the front row. Pitch for the middle of the room. The experts will still learn from how you explain it.
  • One talk, one time. A talk improves on its third outing. Offer it to a second group, local or virtual.
  • Recording surprises. If the leaders record and publish sessions, your screen goes with it. Close email, Slack and every other tab first.

Next

Once you have spoken a few times, the next step is helping run the room: Lead a community covers co-leading a TUG and what the Ambassador and Visionary programmes ask. If you want material for the next talk, an Iron Viz entry makes a good one.

On the brewhouse floor

Brewers already give this talk: the five-minute shift handover where the night brewer explains what happened to the tank that went slow. The same structure works at a user group. The problem is a fermentation that stalled. The build is a dashboard of gravity readings per tank with a target band. The result is the night someone caught a stuck ferment early. The takeaway is the reference band anyone can add. For the build, see a live fermentation-monitoring dashboard.
