Tableau Public, plainly: what it is free for and what it costs you

Tableau Public is a free place to build and share vizzes. Everything you publish there is public, downloadable by default and capped at 10 GB of storage. Here is what that means before you upload anything.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 0 Ankur Napa PublicCommunity

Short answer

Tableau Public is a free website where anyone can build, publish and explore data visualisations. The price is privacy. Every workbook you save there sits on the open internet. By default, anyone can download the workbook and the data inside it. You get 10 GB of storage, extracts only (no live connections) and a cap of 15 million rows. Use it for public data, practice and your portfolio. Never use it for work data, even "just for a minute".

The idea in one picture

There are three ways to make a viz for Tableau Public. A fourth edition of Desktop cannot publish there at all.

ToolCostPublishes to Tableau PublicSaves locallyGood for
Web authoring on public.tableau.com (select Create)FreeYesNoQuick vizzes from a laptop, no install
Tableau Desktop Public EditionFreeYesYesReal learning, offline drafts
Tableau Desktop Professional EditionCreator licenceYesYesPeople who already have a licence
Tableau Desktop Free EditionFreeNoYesPrivate or commercial work you never share

Public Edition is not for commercial use. Free Edition is the newer option for private work: no row cap, more connectors, but it cannot save to Tableau Public, Tableau Cloud or Tableau Server. If someone tells you "Tableau Desktop is free now", ask which edition they mean.

A ten-minute tour

You do not need to build anything yet. Look at how the platform works so the rules make sense.

  1. Open Tableau Public and select a viz from the home page. Look at the toolbar under it. You will usually find Share, Download and a ribbon icon labelled Nominate for Viz of the Day.
  2. Select Download. If the author left downloads on, you can take the whole workbook (a .twbx file) with its data. That is the default for every viz, including yours.
  3. Search for a topic you care about. Sort the results by View count, then filter to vizzes that can be downloaded or copied. Open one and reverse-engineer it in Desktop Public Edition. This is how most of the community learned.
  4. Open an author's profile. Note the Follow button, the favourites count on each viz card and any achievements badges. Achievements come from Viz of the Day, Iron Viz and the Ambassador and Visionary programmes.
  5. Sign up for a free account. Then select your profile icon > Settings. Find the default download setting for future vizzes. Decide now whether new vizzes should be downloadable. On suits portfolio work: the FAQ points out that downloads also open the View Data table, which screen-reader users rely on.
  6. On your profile, notice the empty Hidden tab. Hidden vizzes vanish from your profile but stay reachable by anyone who has the URL. Hidden is not private.
  7. Read the Viz of the Day gallery for a week. It is the Tableau Public team's own taste, with help from Tableau Ambassadors. It is the fastest way to see what "good" looks like this year.

What people get wrong

  • "Hidden means private." It does not. The FAQ says plainly that anyone who knows or stumbles across the URL can see a hidden viz.
  • "I turned off downloads, so my data is safe." Turning off downloads stops the Download button. It does not stop people reading the numbers off your marks, tooltips and View Data. If the data must not be public, it must not be published.
  • "Work data is fine if I anonymise the column names." Customer counts, prices and store names survive renaming. If it came from your employer or a client, it stays off Tableau Public.
  • "Overwriting a viz keeps my views." Saving from Desktop with the same name and choosing to overwrite creates what Tableau Public treats as a brand-new workbook. The view count resets to zero. Renaming or deleting a sheet that people viewed also lowers it.
  • "Tableau Public is a backup." Deleted vizzes cannot be recovered by anyone. Every embed of them breaks. Keep your .twbx files locally. Desktop Public Edition saves locally now, so there is no excuse.
  • "Free Edition and Public Edition are the same." One publishes to the web and is not for commercial use. The other never publishes anywhere.

On the brewhouse floor

Tableau Public is a good home for beer data that is already public. Excise and production figures that a government publishes, style guidelines and brewery counts by state all make honest portfolio vizzes. A brewery's own batch records do not belong there, however harmless they look. Brewhouse yield by batch tells a competitor more about your process than you would say over a pint.
