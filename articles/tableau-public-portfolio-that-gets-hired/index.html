A Tableau Public portfolio that gets you hired

Curate your Tableau Public profile around one audience and three strong vizzes: what to feature, what to hide, how to organise the rest and how to read your own stats.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 2 Ankur Napa PublicCommunityDesign

Short answer

A hiring manager gives your profile about two minutes. Make those two minutes easy. Pick one audience, put three vizzes at the top that each prove a different skill to that audience, write a one-line bio that names the audience. Hide or categorise everything else. Volume does not impress anyone. Fifty challenge entries with no order say "busy". Three finished pieces with descriptions say "hire me".

What you will build

A profile with:

  • a bio and banner that say who you help,
  • one featured viz,
  • three anchor vizzes that each show a different skill,
  • every other viz sorted into categories (once you have ten or more) or hidden as a draft,
  • Available for Hire switched on if you are looking.

The three anchors, for an analyst aiming at retail and distribution roles, could be built on the Harbour Goods sample files (orders, products, stores) or on public data in the same domain:

AnchorProvesExample
An operational dashboardYou can build what a team opens every MondayNet sales by store and month, with a filter action from store to product
An analysis with a findingYou can find something and say it in one sentenceWhich categories lean hardest on discounts and what that costs
A technique pieceYou understand Tableau, not just Show MeA parameter-driven what-if or a set action drill-down

Before you start

  • At least five published vizzes. If you have fewer, go back to your first community challenge for a few weeks.
  • A decision about the audience. "Data people" is not an audience. "Retail operations managers in India" is.
  • A local copy of every workbook you plan to change. Overwriting resets view counts.

Steps

1. Write the bio for the audience

  1. Select your profile icon > My Profile > Edit Profile.
  2. Write a bio of one or two lines. Lead with who you help and how: "I build Tableau dashboards for retail and distribution teams: store performance, stock and discount analysis."
  3. Add title, location and links to LinkedIn and any site where you write about your work.
  4. Tick Available for Hire and confirm the email address enquiries should go to. Select Save.
  5. Select Customize Banner and pick a colour and pattern that match your vizzes rather than fight them.

2. Choose and polish the three anchors

For each anchor:

  1. Open it in Desktop Public Edition from your local copy.
  2. Make the title a finding. "Discounts doubled in Kolkata East and margin fell with them" beats "Discount Analysis".
  3. Add a short caption or text box that says where the data came from. For invented data, say so.
  4. Check contrast: text at 4.5:1 against its background, 3:1 for large text. Use a colour-blind-safe palette and add labels or shapes so colour is not the only cue.
  5. Add alt text where you have images. Tableau Public's accessibility guidance asks for descriptive titles, legends and filters as well.
  6. Publish with the same workbook name and only if you accept the view count reset. Otherwise publish a new version and hide the old one.
  7. On the viz page select Edit Details and write two to three sentences: the question, the data source, the finding.

3. Feature one viz

On your profile, hover over the strongest anchor and select it as your featured viz. It sits at the top of the profile, so it is the first thing anyone sees. Choose the operational dashboard if you want analyst roles. Choose the analysis if you want roles that write and present.

4. Organise the rest

Once you have ten or more visible vizzes, select Organize Your Vizzes on your profile.

  1. Set Organize by to Categories.
  2. Add your own category names, six words or fewer each. Name them for the reader: Retail dashboards, Analysis, Techniques, Community challenges.
  3. Use Add Vizzes on each category to assign vizzes. A viz can sit in several categories but must sit in at least one.
  4. Drag categories into the order you want. Recent and Other cannot be renamed or deleted.
  5. Select Save Organization.

There is also an Auto-Categorize button that uses AI in Tableau to suggest category names and sort your vizzes. It is a quick first draft. Know that every time you select it, it resets your previous organisation, manual or automatic. Run it once at most, then edit by hand.

5. Hide drafts, not mistakes

Use the Hidden tab for work in progress. Hidden vizzes disappear from your profile but stay reachable by URL. Delete anything that should not exist at all; but remember a deleted viz cannot be recovered and every embed of it breaks.

6. Put the portfolio where hiring happens

  1. Use Share on each anchor to copy its link or embed code.
  2. Link the profile in your LinkedIn headline section and your CV. Link one anchor viz per role you apply for, matched to the job.
  3. When you are proud of a piece, nominate it with Nominate for Viz of the Day. Self-nomination is allowed. Nominating does not guarantee anything; it puts the viz in front of the people who choose.

Check it worked

Do this audit from a private browser window, signed out, with a timer set to two minutes:

CheckPass when
AudienceThe bio names who you help in the first line
First impressionThe featured viz loads and its title states a finding
RangeThe three anchors show three different skills
ContextEvery anchor has a description with the data source
OrderCategories exist (10+ vizzes) and challenge entries are not mixed with anchors
ContactHire Me shows, if you are looking
AccessibilityText contrast holds, colour is never the only cue

Then read your Stats tab once a month. It shows the last 30 days against the previous 30 for Viewed, Favorited, Inspired and Followed. Inspired is the one to watch: it counts people who credited your work as their inspiration. That is a portfolio doing its job.

Where this breaks

  • The portfolio has no audience. Twelve unrelated topics read as a hobby. That is fine for learning but not for hiring.
  • Work data creeps in. One "anonymised" employer dashboard can cost you more than a hundred good vizzes earn. Keep to public or invented data.
  • Downloads off everywhere. It feels protective. It also hides the View Data table that screen-reader users need and stops other people learning from you.
  • Auto-Categorize after hand-sorting. It wipes your manual organisation. Delete existing categories deliberately if you want fresh AI suggestions.
  • Republishing to "tidy up". Overwriting, renaming sheets or deleting viewed sheets lowers view counts. Polish once, properly.
  • Chasing Viz of the Day. It is a nice moment. It is not a strategy. Hiring managers look for relevance to their problem.

Next on the roadmap

When the portfolio has a strong spine, test it in public at Iron Viz. Or turn one anchor into a talk with Speak at a user group. Both make your profile easier to find.

On the brewhouse floor

A portfolio built around one industry is easier to hire from than a portfolio built around ten. Beer, whisky and wine are good themes because so much of their data is public: harvest reports, appellation rules, excise statistics, style guidelines. Three anchors could be a winery's direct-to-consumer sales dashboard, an analysis of vintage quality against weather and a parameter-driven blending what-if. For worked examples, see a wine DTC and varietal sales dashboard and a cask maturation dashboard.
