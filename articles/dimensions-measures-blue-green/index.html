Blue pills and green pills: dimensions and measures

Every field in Tableau is a dimension or a measure. Every pill is discrete (blue) or continuous (green). Learn the two ideas apart, because the colour decides what Tableau draws.

12 Oct 2026 Tableau as of October 2026 Checked against Tableau Help Level 0 Ankur Napa DesktopCertification

Short answer

Tableau sorts every field two ways. A dimension is something you group by, like a store or a product. A measure is a number you add up, like quantity. Separately, every field you drop in the view is discrete or continuous. Discrete pills are blue and draw headers, one label per value. Continuous pills are green and draw an axis, a line of numbers with no gaps. Most dimensions are blue and most measures are green, which is why people mix the two ideas up. They are not the same thing. The exam tests the difference.

The idea in one picture

A pill is the rounded tag a field becomes when you drag it onto a shelf. Its colour tells you how Tableau will draw it.

Discrete (blue)Continuous (green)
DimensionStore Id as headers: ST-003, ST-007, ST-011A date as a timeline axis: July to September as one line
MeasureUnit Price turned discrete: 549, 699, 899 as headersSUM(Quantity) as an axis from 0 upwards

Four words to hold on to:

  • Dimension. Qualitative. It describes. It splits the data into groups. In the Data pane it sits above the line.
  • Measure. Quantitative. You can add, average or count it. It sits below the line in the Data pane.
  • Discrete. Separate, countable values. Tableau draws a header for each one.
  • Continuous. An unbroken range. Tableau draws an axis.

And one more: aggregation. When a measure lands in the view, Tableau does not show every row. It rolls the rows up, by default with SUM. That is why the pill reads SUM(Quantity) and not Quantity. Add a dimension to the view and the sum is cut into one value per member of that dimension.

No dimension in the view         Add Store Id (blue)
SUM(Quantity) = 104              ST-003   27
                                 ST-014   21
                                 ST-019   20
                                 ...      ...

Same measure, same rows, a different question. That is the whole trick behind Tableau.

A ten-minute tour

You need Tableau Desktop (any edition) and harbour-goods-orders.csv. It holds 60 order rows for Harbour Goods, a fictional retailer with six stores.

  1. On the start page, under Connect > To a File, select Text file and open the CSV. Then select Sheet 1 at the bottom left.
  2. Look at the Data pane. Tableau tidies the column names, so order_date shows as Order Date and store_id as Store Id. Your version may capitalise them slightly differently. Above the grey line sit Order Id, Order Date, Product Id and Store Id: dimensions. Below it sit Discount, Quantity and Unit Price: measures. Tableau treats a column ending in "id" as a dimension even when it holds numbers.
  3. Drag Quantity to the Rows shelf. The pill turns green and reads SUM(Quantity). You get one bar. Hover over it: 104.
  4. Drag Store Id to Columns. The pill is blue. You now get one bar per store and a header under each bar. ST-003 shows 27, the biggest. There is also an ST-099. Remember it: it is not a real store. A later step deals with it.
  5. Right-click (Control-click on Mac) SUM(Quantity) on Rows and point to Measure (Sum). The list shows the other aggregations: Average, Median, Count, Minimum, Maximum and more. Pick Average. The bars now show average quantity per order line. Change it back to Sum.
  6. Drag Unit Price from the Data pane to Columns, to the left of Store Id. It comes in green as SUM(Unit Price). The view turns into a mess of axes. Undo with Ctrl+Z (Command+Z on Mac).
  7. Now right-click Unit Price in the Data pane and select Convert to Discrete. Its icon turns blue. Drag it to Columns again. Now you get a header per price point: 549, 699, 899 and so on. Same field, different colour, completely different chart. Convert it back with Convert to Continuous.
  8. Drag Order Date to Columns in place of Store Id. It arrives blue as YEAR(Order Date): a header for 2026 and a Null header for the one row dated 31 September, a day that does not exist. Dates come in discrete by default. The dates step on the roadmap shows how to make them green.

What people get wrong

  • "Blue means dimension and green means measure." Close, but wrong. Blue means discrete. Green means continuous. A measure can be blue (step 7) and a date dimension can be green.
  • "The bar shows quantity." It shows SUM(Quantity) at the level of the dimensions in the view. Remove Store Id and the same pill shows 104.
  • "Numbers are measures." Not always. An ID, a postcode or a year is a number you should never add up. Drag it above the line in the Data pane to make it a dimension.
  • "The total looks too big." Usually a measure that should be averaged is being summed, like Unit Price. Change the aggregation, or ask whether the field belongs in the view at all.
  • "Null quantity is zero." One row in the sample has no quantity. SUM ignores it, but COUNT and AVG treat it differently. The calculated fields step shows how ZN() fills it in.

On the brewhouse floor

In a brewery the fermenter number is a dimension and the gravity reading is a measure. Put FV-03 on Columns and SUM(Gravity) on Rows and Tableau adds every reading together, which means nothing. Change the aggregation to Average, or better, put the reading date on Columns as a continuous date so each tank draws its own falling line. The tank number is a number painted on steel. It is still a label, not a quantity, so it belongs above the line.
