Tableau in beer, whisky and wine

I was an R&D Master Brewer for more than eight years before I built my first dashboard. Most dashboards in this trade do not die because of the tool. They die because the view does not match how the plant runs. This page is the roadmap seen from the floor: the gap in each trade, the Tableau technique that closes it and thirty builds you can copy.

Beer

A brewery is a flow: grain to mash to wort to fermenter to bright tank to keg. Build the dashboard like a retail ledger and you lose the chain. The brewers open it twice, then go back to the clipboard on the tank.

Speak the units: Degrees Plato, hectolitres, EBC colour, IBU, apparent attenuation, brewhouse yield.

Whisky and spirits

Spirits are counted in litres of pure alcohol, mature for years and are taxed on what leaves bond. Mix bulk litres with pure alcohol or lose one cask move. Both the yield and the stock valuation will be wrong for the next decade.

Speak the units: Litres of pure alcohol, ABV, cut points, angel's share, cask age, LPA per tonne.

On the floorIn TableauLearn it
Track every cask through refills, moves and vatting One cask-level source with relationships to fills and movements, a FIXED LOD for current age and strength
  • Level 2: Relationships and multi-fact models
  • Level 2: LOD expressions
See where in the warehouse the angel takes more than its share A rack position grid as a highlight table, temperature and loss on colour, Explain Data on the outliers
  • Level 2: Charts beyond Show Me
  • Level 2: Tableau Agent and Explain Data
Value maturing stock the way finance signs it off One certified published data source for stock, used by finance and production alike
  • Level 2: Published data sources and refresh schedules
  • Level 4: One KPI, one definition
Try a vatting before anyone draws a sample Parameter actions that swap candidate casks into a blend and recompute strength and sensory scores

Wine

Wine runs on vintage time and a lot chain from block to pick to tank to barrel to blend. Count tonnes of fruit against finished litres or drop the chain. No winemaker will trust the yield after that.

Speak the units: Brix, tonnes per hectare, YAN, volatile acidity, free SO2, litres per tonne.

On the floorIn TableauLearn it
Know which block to pick first Blocks on a map with ripeness on colour, Brix and acidity curves per block, a Pulse digest during harvest
  • Level 1: Maps with geographic roles
  • Level 1: Your first Pulse metric
Spot a ferment heading for trouble Per-tank Brix and temperature lines, a stuck-ferment flag as a calculated field, Explain Data on the odd tank
  • Level 1: Calculated fields
  • Level 2: Tableau Agent and Explain Data
Let growers see their own fruit and nobody else's Row-level security with an entitlements table on Tableau Server or Cloud
  • Level 2: Row-level security
  • Level 3: Sites, projects and the permission model
Compare cellar door and club sales with distribution Varietal and vintage by channel, a filled map of markets, sets for club members in or out
  • Level 2: Sets and set actions
  • Level 1: Maps with geographic roles
