Which chart when

Start from the question, not the chart. Pick what you are trying to say and you get the charts that say it, when each one goes wrong and the shelf recipe to build it.

Discrete blue pills draw headers. Continuous green pills draw axes.

Compare categories

For example: Which hop variety cost us most this year?

  • Bar chart

    Use it for Comparing a measure across categories. Sort it and the ranking comes free.

    Skip it when Not for long time series, where a line reads better, or when the axis cannot start at zero.

Charts to avoid or use carefully

Every one of these has a place. The place is smaller than people think.

  • Pie beyond a few slices

    The trouble Angles are hard to compare. Two or three slices with one clear winner is fine; seven slices is a guessing game.

    Try instead A sorted bar chart, or a 100 percent stacked bar for share.

    Tableau Help: Build a pie chart

  • 3D charts

    The trouble Tableau does not draw them, for good reason. Perspective makes near bars look bigger than far ones and hides the values behind.

    Try instead The flat version of the same chart. If someone asks for 3D, ask what they want to see.

  • Dual-axis traps

    The trouble Two independent axes can be stretched until any two lines cross where you like. Unsynchronised axes on the same unit are worse.

    Try instead Synchronise axes when the units match, label both clearly, or use two small charts stacked.

    Tableau Help: Add axes for multiple measures

  • Packed bubbles

    The trouble They look friendly and say very little. Circle area is the weakest common encoding and the layout has no order.

    Try instead A bar chart or treemap. Keep bubbles for a decorative cover sheet, if at all.

    Tableau Help: Build a packed bubble chart
