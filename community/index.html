Why user groups matter

Documentation tells you what a feature does. A user group tells you what happened when someone shipped it on a Monday with real data and a manager waiting. That second story is where the learning is.

It is the same on a brewery floor. The recipe sheet says ferment at 18 degrees. The brewer next to you tells you the probe on tank three reads two degrees low. You need both.

Join the Tableau community in six steps

  1. Make a Trailblazer account

    Your free Trailhead login is the key to Tableau certification and the Tableau forums on the Trailblazer Community. Note that the online Trailblazer Community becomes read-only on 30 October 2026, with new conversations moving to Slack.

  2. Join a Tableau User Group

    Search the user group directory by city or topic and press Join. Start with your local group and one virtual group, such as the Tableau Newbies User Group if you are new.

  3. Join the Tableau Community Slack

    This is where the DataFam talks every day. Open the Tableau Community Slack page, press Join today and accept the invitation with your email. About 23,800 members were in it when I checked in October 2026. Say hello in your user group's channel, share work in #community-share and look at #job-board. Stuck? Message a leader of your user group, or a Tableau Ambassador: the 2026 class is listed with their interests in a dashboard linked from the Ambassador announcement.

  4. Follow #DataFam

    Follow the #DataFam tag on the social network you already use. Read the monthly DataFam Roundup on the Tableau blog. It is the quickest way to find people worth learning from. I keep a list on the people to follow page.

  5. Enter a challenge

    Pick one of the community projects, such as Workout Wednesday, Back 2 Viz Basics or Preppin' Data. Publish your answer on Tableau Public. The feedback is the point, not the viz.

  6. Speak or volunteer

    Offer your user group a twenty-minute talk on one thing you built. If speaking is not for you yet, volunteer: hosting, notes, finding speakers. Every group I know is short of helpers.

How to become a TUG co-lead

To join an existing group's leadership team, talk to its current leaders: the same rules apply as for a new group. To start a new one, Tableau asks that you:

  • Have at least six months of experience with any Tableau product.
  • Have attended at least one Tableau User Group event.
  • Apply with at least one co-leader. Leaders cannot all be from the same company.
  • Live or work where the group meets. Check that no active group exists for that city.
  • Join the Tableau Community Slack and run at least four events a year.

Students and Salesforce or Tableau employees cannot apply. The current window is 1 to 31 October 2026, on the Start a Group page.

The Tableau Ambassador programme

Ambassadors are community leaders named for a one-year term. The 2026 class has 357 people from 48 countries. Each one carries one of five designations:

DesignationWhat they do
Tableau Community LeaderContributes across the whole community: evangelises Tableau, helps people grow and connects them.
DataDev Community LeaderBuilds on the developer platform with Tableau APIs and shares the technical know-how.
Teaching Community LeaderEducators who support and advise the Tableau Academic Programs.
Student Community LeaderStudents who run on-campus events and social campaigns for the Academic Programs.
CRM Analytics Community LeaderPower users of CRM Analytics who bridge the Salesforce Trailblazer and Tableau communities.

Everyone applies again each year, returning Ambassadors included. The 2026 window ran from 10 June to 16 July 2026. For the first time you could apply or nominate through the Community Slack. Only public contributions count: work done inside your own company does not. Ambassadors get software and eLearning access and sign an NDA with Tableau.

The Visionary programme

Visionaries are Tableau's other community leadership programme: people who show mastery of the product, teach in the community and work with Tableau to make it better. The 2026 class has 41 Visionaries from 15 countries, each on a one-year term. A committee of Tableau employees scores applicants on teaching, mastery and collaboration, counting only public, free-to-access contributions from the previous year. Applications for 2026 closed on 20 January 2026. Visionaries who serve at least five terms can be named to the Visionary Hall of Fame.
